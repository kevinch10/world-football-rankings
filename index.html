      How it works

      Official vs live

      FIFA publishes the official men's world ranking a few times a year. Between releases, this site takes the latest official points and adds the result of every international match played since, using FIFA's own formula. That's the live rank. The official rank is the one FIFA last published.

      How points change

      After each match, each team gains or loses points depending on the result, the strength of the opponent, and how much the match counts (its weight). Beating a stronger team earns more; losing to a weaker one costs more. Both teams' changes always add up to zero, except in knockout rounds of a final tournament, where the losing team keeps its points.

      Match weights

      Friendly outside an international window5
      Friendly in a window, or a regional cup10
      Nations League (league phase and play-offs)15
      Nations League finals, World Cup and continental qualifiers25
      Continental finals (EURO, AFCON, Asian Cup…): before / from quarter-finals35 / 40
      World Cup finals: before / from quarter-finals50 / 60

      Points at stake

      For upcoming matches, the numbers show what each team would gain or lose for a win, draw or loss, based on today's live points.

      Where the data comes from

      Rankings, results and fixtures come from FIFA's public data, updated about every hour. A few competitions FIFA's feed doesn't carry (such as AFCON qualifiers) come from Wikipedia and a community results dataset; those matches are labelled. Rarely, FIFA changes a result after the match (a forfeit, for example). The live rank can then differ slightly from the next official ranking.
